# Fiqh Notes Rules

## Main Source Rules

- Use the lecture transcript as the primary source.
- Use the provided Fiqh course PDF/material only as a supporting reference, especially when the transcript is unclear.
- Do not add outside information unless explicitly requested.
- If a point is only from the PDF/material and not clearly from the lecture, either omit it or clearly mark it as supporting material.
- Do not invent or assume rulings, conditions, exceptions, amounts, time periods, or sequences.
- If the transcript is corrupted or unclear, avoid presenting unclear wording as certain.

## Content Selection Rules

- Omit opening revision unless it contains new information needed for the notes.
- Do not mention that opening revision was omitted.
- Include important:
  - definitions
  - rulings
  - conditions
  - exceptions
  - evidences
  - amounts, time periods, or counts when relevant
  - sequence/order
  - examples
  - differences of opinion when mentioned
  - stronger/correct view when the lecture mentions it
- Do not over-compress technical Fiqh sections.
- Preserve numbered lists when the Shaikh gives numbered lists.
- Avoid redundant repetition.
- For repeated technical rulings, state the ruling clearly once unless the lecture adds a new angle.

## Wording And Style Rules

- Use **Shaikh said** rarely and only when attribution is needed.
- Do not use "the teacher said."
- Do not use "the lecture mentions" unless necessary.
- Prefer direct notes instead of constant attribution.
- Use **The Prophet (PBUH)**.
- Use **Shariah** with final **h**.
- Use plain transliteration without diacritics.
- Translate Urdu/Arabic into English unless the term is a core Islamic term.
- Keep core Islamic terms when appropriate, such as:
  - Salah
  - Shariah
  - wajib
  - Sunnah
  - mustahabb
  - haram
  - makruh
- Notes should be concise without losing important technical detail.

## Styling Rules

- Styling inside bullet points is allowed when useful.
- You may bold important:
  - terms
  - rulings
  - conditions
  - exceptions
  - keywords
- Do not overuse bolding.
- Headings should be bold.

## Hadith / Evidence Rules

- Include relevant Qur'an/Hadith evidence when it supports the ruling.
- Do not overload notes with long full narrations unless necessary.
- Hadith and stories should usually stay grouped rather than being split into many tiny points.
- If a Hadith is weak and the Shaikh says so, mention that it is weak and avoid presenting it as proof.
- If a narration is described as fabricated or weak, preserve that classification.

## Fiqh Accuracy Rules

- Fiqh notes must be especially precise.
- Do not lose:
  - who a ruling applies to
  - whether something is wajib, Sunnah, haram, makruh, allowed, or not established
  - whether something is a condition or an excuse/barrier
  - exact amounts, time periods, counts, or thresholds when mentioned
  - whether a requirement or condition applies or does not apply
  - whether an act is valid but sinful, invalid, or not established
- If something is an exemption, do not write it as a positive condition.
- If something is a barrier or excuse, label it clearly.
- Preserve structured technical material such as:
  - conditions
  - pillars
  - obligations
  - categories
  - exceptions
  - sequences
  - classifications
  - numbered rulings

## Specific Past Corrections To Preserve

- Do not include opening revision unless it adds new information.
- Put a proper topic heading under the lecture number.
- Use **The Prophet (PBUH)**.
- Use **Shaikh**, not "teacher."
- Do not silently add PDF-only details into lecture notes.
- Avoid uncertain extrapolations or rulings that were not clearly stated.
- If the distinction between lecture content and PDF content matters, state it.
