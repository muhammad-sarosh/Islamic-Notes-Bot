# Shared Islamic Notes Rules

## General Content Rules

- Follow the lecture transcript first.
- Preserve the lecture's original order as closely as possible.
- Do not move important points far earlier or later than where they appear in the lecture.
- Never invent, assume, or add information just to expand the notes.
- Check the entire transcript carefully before finalizing.
- Do not omit important definitions, conditions, exceptions, distinctions, examples, lists, quotations, or sequence details.
- Preserve meaningful side-details, examples, and clarifications when they help explain a ruling or concept, but omit conversational tangents that do not add substantive information.
- Condense repetition without overcompressing important material.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.
- Do not split one connected idea into too many small bullets.
- Omit revision from previous lectures unless new information is added.
- Omit admin talk, exam details, breaks, and unrelated casual conversation.
- Do not include uncertainty or personal commentary inside the finished notes.
- If something remains uncertain, flag it separately instead of presenting it as established.
- Include relevant Qur'an verses, Hadith, stories, and quotes mentioned in the lecture.
- Keep each Hadith or story mostly in one bullet unless it becomes too long or awkward.
- Preserve explicitly numbered categories, points, and sequences in their original order.

## Language Rules

- Write notes in clear, concise English.
- Translate Urdu and nonessential Arabic terms into clear English.
- Retain established Islamic terms where appropriate.
- Use plain-letter transliteration without special transliteration symbols.
- Write `Shariah` with the final `h`.
- Write `The Prophet (PBUH)`.
- Never write "the teacher" or "the author."
- Use `Shaikh said` only when attribution is genuinely necessary.
- Do not overuse `Shaikh said`.
- State points directly when attribution is unnecessary.
- Retain established Islamic and technical Arabic terms when they are standard terms of the subject. For example, use `sahih`, `hasan`, `da'if`, and `mawdu'` rather than translating them as "sound", "fair", "weak", or "fabricated" when referring to formal hadith classifications. 

## Structure Rules

- Begin with the required lecture-number heading.
- Directly below the lecture heading, add a short description of the lecture's subject.
- Use bold headings.
- Do not number headings inside the actual notes.
- Use clear headings that reflect the actual topic being discussed.
- Keep headings in the same sequence as the lecture.
- Keep connected information together instead of making every sentence a separate bullet.

## Final Check Rules

- Compare the completed notes against the full transcript before finalizing.
- Confirm that headings and content follow the lecture's original order.
- Check that important material has not been moved significantly earlier or later.
- Ensure every numbered list contains all items mentioned.
- Ensure Hadith and stories have not been unnecessarily fragmented.
- Confirm that no revision-only material, admin discussion, or unsupported information has been included.
- Verify that uncertainty has been flagged separately, not written as fact inside the notes.
