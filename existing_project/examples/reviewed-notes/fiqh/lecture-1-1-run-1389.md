Marriage: Its Meaning, Rulings, Purposes, and Choosing a Wife

**Meaning of Nikah**

- Linguistically, *nikah* means joining, mingling, or coming together. It is used, for example, when trees grow intertwined or rainwater is absorbed into the earth.
- In Islamic usage, it has two meanings:
  - The marriage contract: an agreement between husband and wife that permits marital relations and serves purposes such as having children and establishing a household.
  - Marital relations themselves. In the verse about a woman becoming lawful to her first husband after marrying another, “until she marries another husband” means that the marriage must be consummated; the contract alone is not enough (Qur’an 2:230).
- A marriage arranged solely to make a woman lawful again to her first husband is not a valid marriage according to the lecture. Intimacy in such an arrangement is unlawful, and it does not make her lawful to her first husband. A valid second marriage is intended as a lasting marriage, even if it later ends through divorce or the husband’s death.

**Evidence for the Legitimacy of Marriage**

- Marriage is established by the Qur’an, Sunnah, and the consensus of the Muslim community. The Qur’an says, “Marry women who are pleasing to you” (Qur’an 4:3), and commands guardians to arrange marriages for the unmarried among them (Qur’an 24:32).
- The Prophet (PBUH) told young people that whoever has the financial ability to marry should do so, because marriage helps lower the gaze and protect chastity. Whoever cannot afford it should fast, as fasting restrains sexual desire.
- The Prophet (PBUH) also encouraged marrying women who are loving and likely to have children, saying that he would take pride in the large number of his community on the Day of Resurrection. The lecture states that Muslims agree on the legitimacy of marriage.

**Purposes and Wisdoms of Marriage**

- The lecture listed these purposes:
  1. Worshipping Allah and obeying The Prophet (PBUH). Marital relations can be an act of charity when pursued lawfully to avoid what is forbidden. The Prophet (PBUH) also said that marriage is part of his Sunnah.
  2. Establishing tranquility, love, mercy, and stability between spouses (Qur’an 30:21).
  3. Fulfilling sexual desire in a lawful way and protecting chastity.
  4. Preserving lineage and family relationships, and preventing disorder in family life. The lecture stressed the seriousness of falsely attributing oneself to someone other than one’s father.
  5. Preserving the human race through families and the upbringing of children.
  6. Increasing the Muslim community. The Prophet (PBUH) encouraged marrying loving, fertile women, saying he would take pride in the community’s numbers on the Day of Resurrection.
  7. Establishing a righteous society through family ties, mutual rights, and the upbringing of people.
  8. Cultivating good character and protecting people from adultery and other immoral conduct.

**The Ruling on Marriage**

- The ruling differs according to a person’s circumstances. The five legal categories are: obligatory (fard or wajib), Sunnah (mustahabb), permissible, makruh, and haram.
- Marriage is generally Sunnah or mustahabb for someone who has sexual desire, can afford its expenses, and does not fear falling into unlawful relations. Some scholars consider it obligatory for such a person; the lecture identifies the majority view as Sunnah.
- Marriage is obligatory for someone who fears falling into zina, provided they can meet the responsibilities and expenses of marriage. If they cannot afford it, they should fast and remain chaste until Allah grants them provision.
- Marriage is makruh for someone who has no need for it, such as a person without sexual desire due to impotence, old age, or illness, or a poor person without sexual desire. The reason given is that they may face difficulties without being able to fulfil their spouse’s rights. Some scholars consider it haram where inability to fulfil those rights could lead to harm or wrongdoing; the lecture gives makruh as the primary ruling.
- Marriage is haram for a person in a war zone (*Dar al-Harb*), as described in the lecture, and for someone who knows they will not treat their wives fairly if they marry more than one.

**Choosing a Wife**

- It is Sunnah to choose a woman who is religious, chaste, and good in character. The Hadith describes four considerations people may have when marrying: her wealth, family standing, beauty, and religion. The Prophet (PBUH) instructed people to prioritize a woman’s religion.
- Religion and character are distinct considerations: outward religious practice alone does not establish good character. The lecture emphasized looking for both.
- It is also Sunnah to choose a woman who is loving and likely to have children. The lecture attributed to Shaikh Ibn Uthaymeen the explanation that a woman’s likely fertility may be inferred from her family background. It cited the blessing of increased numbers among earlier communities, including the people of Shu’ayb (Qur’an 7:86).
- The lecture stated that permanent sterilization is haram, except where a reliable medical report establishes that pregnancy would certainly threaten the woman’s life. If repeated pregnancies cause physical harm or prevent the children’s rights and upbringing from being properly attended to, a suitable interval between pregnancies is permissible.
- As evidence for spacing pregnancies, the lecture cited the Companions’ practice of withdrawal (*azl*) while revelation was descending, which the Prophet (PBUH) did not prohibit. The lecture presented this as evidence that an act known to occur during the period of revelation and not prohibited was permissible.
- Choosing a virgin is also Sunnah. The Prophet (PBUH) asked Jabir why he had not married a virgin, so they could enjoy one another’s companionship. A previously married woman may be preferable when there is a particular benefit: Jabir explained that after his father died, he wanted a mature wife who could help care for his sisters.
- Alongside religion and good character, beauty may be considered where possible, as it can contribute to comfort, affection, and lowering the gaze. Beauty should not be the sole basis for choosing a wife.
